Storage Devices

Storage is where a computer keeps your files, apps, and operating system, even when the power is off. From spinning hard drives to lightning-fast NVMe SSDs and the cloud, each type trades off speed, capacity, cost, and portability. This guide covers the main storage devices, how saving a file actually works, how capacity is measured, and how to keep your data safe.

Where Your Data Lives

Types of Storage

Storage (also called secondary storage) is non-volatile, which means it keeps data without power. Use the buttons above to filter by where the storage sits: inside the computer, carried around, or in the cloud. Speeds and sizes shown are typical ranges and change as technology improves.

Internal
💽

💽 Hard Disk Drive (HDD)

An HDD stores data magnetically on fast-spinning platters. A tiny read/write head floats just above the surface to read and write bits. It is cheap for the amount of space, but it has moving parts, so it is slower and more sensitive to drops.

How it storesMagnetized spots on spinning metal platters
Typical capacity500 GB to 20+ TB
Typical speedAbout 80–200 MB/s
Best forCheap bulk storage and backups
Internal
🗄️

🗄️ SATA Solid-State Drive (SSD)

A SATA SSD has no moving parts. It keeps data in flash memory chips, so it starts fast, runs quietly, and survives bumps better than an HDD. It uses the same SATA connection older hard drives use, which limits its speed.

How it storesElectrical charge trapped in flash memory cells
Typical capacity250 GB to 4 TB
Typical speedAbout 500–550 MB/s
Best forSpeeding up an older laptop or desktop
Internal

⚡ NVMe M.2 SSD

An NVMe SSD is a small stick that plugs straight into the motherboard and talks to the system over PCIe lanes, which are much faster than SATA. Most new laptops and gaming PCs use one for the operating system and apps.

How it storesFlash memory chips on a small M.2 stick
Typical capacity250 GB to 4+ TB
Typical speedRoughly 3,000–7,000+ MB/s
Best forOperating system, games, and video editing
Portable
🔌

🔌 USB Flash Drive

A flash drive is a thumb-sized stick of flash memory with a USB plug. It needs no battery or power supply and works on almost any computer, which makes it great for carrying school files.

How it storesFlash memory chips inside a plastic or metal case
Typical capacity16 GB to 1 TB
Typical speedAbout 30 to 400+ MB/s, depending on USB version
Best forMoving files between computers
Portable
📷

📷 SD and microSD Cards

SD cards use flash memory in a stamp-sized card. Cameras, drones, handheld game consoles and phones use them to add storage. Speed ratings printed on the card show how fast it can write video.

How it storesFlash memory in a thin removable card
Typical capacity16 GB to about 1 TB
Typical speedAbout 10 to 300 MB/s, depending on class
Best forCameras, phones, and game consoles
Portable
🧳

🧳 External Drive

An external drive is an HDD or SSD in its own case that connects by USB. It gives you a big, portable place for backups and large files like photos and videos without opening the computer.

How it storesAn HDD or SSD inside a USB-connected case
Typical capacity500 GB to 20+ TB
Typical speedAbout 100 MB/s (HDD) to 1,000+ MB/s (SSD)
Best forBackups and large media libraries
Portable
📀

📀 Optical Disc (CD / DVD / Blu-ray)

Optical discs store data as tiny pits and flat areas on a shiny surface that a laser reads. They are cheap and last a long time if kept safe, but they hold little, work slowly, and many new computers no longer include a disc drive.

How it storesPits and lands read by a laser
Typical capacityCD about 700 MB, DVD 4.7 GB, Blu-ray 25 GB per layer
Typical speedSlow compared with drives
Best forMovies, music, and long-term archives
Cloud
☁️

☁️ Cloud Storage

Cloud storage keeps your files on a company’s servers and lets you reach them from any device with an internet connection. Services like Google Drive, OneDrive, iCloud and Dropbox also back up your files and make sharing easy.

How it storesData on remote servers in data centers
Typical capacityFree tiers of several GB; paid plans from about 100 GB to several TB
Typical speedDepends on your internet connection
Best forAccess anywhere, sharing, and automatic backup

Two Kinds of Computer Memory

RAM vs Storage

People often mix up memory and storage. A good picture: RAM is your desk (the workspace for what you are using right now) and storage is your filing cabinet (where everything is kept for later).

Primary · Volatile

RAM (Memory)

  • Very fast working space for open apps and files
  • Erased when the computer shuts off
  • Smaller: usually 8–32 GB on everyday computers
  • More RAM lets you run more things at once
Secondary · Non-volatile

Storage (Drives)

  • Keeps the operating system, apps, and files long-term
  • Data stays when the power is off
  • Larger: hundreds of GB to several TB
  • Slower than RAM, but much cheaper per gigabyte

The Memory Hierarchy

Faster memory costs more and is smaller, so computers layer different kinds, from fastest to slowest.

1
⚡

CPU Cache

Tiny and the fastest. Holds the data the CPU needs right now.

2
🧠

RAM

Fast working memory. Holds open apps and files while you use them. Erased at shutdown.

3
💽

SSD / HDD

Your main storage. Holds the OS, apps and files permanently.

4
☁️

Cloud / Archive

Biggest and slowest. Holds backups and files you rarely open.

Behind the Save Button

How Saving a File Works

Every time you save a document or photo, the same five steps happen in a split second.

1
🖱️

You Click Save

An app hands your file to the operating system and asks for it to be stored.

2
📂

File System Organizes

The OS picks free space on the drive and records the file name and location in an index.

3
🎛️

Controller Writes

The drive’s controller turns the data into magnetic patterns (HDD) or electrical charges in flash cells (SSD).

4
💾

Data Stays Saved

Because storage is non-volatile, the bits stay put even when the power is off.

5
📥

Opened Later

When you open the file, the drive copies it into RAM so the CPU can work on it quickly.

Measuring Data

Capacity Units

Computers store everything as bits, which are 1s and 0s. Bigger units group bits together so large amounts of data are easier to talk about.

UnitSizeEveryday Example
BitOne 0 or 1The smallest piece of data
Byte (B)8 bitsOne text character, like the letter “A”
Kilobyte (KB)1,000 bytesA short text message or note
Megabyte (MB)1,000 KBA photo or a song is a few MB
Gigabyte (GB)1,000 MBAn HD movie is roughly 1–5 GB
Terabyte (TB)1,000 GBHundreds of movies or tens of thousands of photos
Why does my 500 GB drive show less space? Drive makers count 1 GB as 1,000,000,000 bytes, but many operating systems count in 1,024-based units. A drive sold as 500 GB shows up as about 465 GB, and some space is also used by the file system and system files.

Protect Your Work

Backup & Care

Every storage device can fail, get lost, or get stolen. A backup is a second copy of your files, and it is the best protection against losing a school project.

The 3-2-1 Rule

Keep Three Copies

  • 3 total copies of important files
  • 2 different types of storage (for example, your laptop and an external drive)
  • 1 copy somewhere else, such as cloud storage
Good Habits

Take Care of Your Drives

  • Use Eject before unplugging a flash or external drive
  • Keep drives away from water, heat, and strong drops
  • Use a strong password and turn on device encryption
  • Replace old drives before they fail
Warning Signs

A Drive May Be Failing If…

  • It makes clicking or grinding sounds
  • Files go missing or won’t open
  • The computer freezes when saving or opening files
  • Copying files becomes very slow

If you see these signs, copy your files off the drive right away.
